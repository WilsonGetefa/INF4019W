Systems Theory views an organization as a collection of interconnected components that work together to achieve a goal. 
A problem in one component can affect the entire system.

The theory suggests that security incidents are not caused by one factor alone but by interactions between different parts of the system.

A simple way to distinguish these theories is to ask:
>   "What is the main thing this theory pays attention to?"

| Theory                              | Focuses On                                                              | Core Idea                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|   Systems Thinking                  | The entire cybersecurity system and the relationships between its parts | Security outcomes result from interactions among people, technology, processes, policies, and the environment.  |
|   Socio-Technical Systems Theory    | The relationship between people and technology                          | Security is achieved when human needs and technical controls are aligned.                                       |
|   Activity Theory                   | Human activities, tasks, and work practices                             | Security behavior is shaped by how people use tools within rules, communities, and organizational contexts.     |
|   General Deterrence Theory (GDT)   | Human behavior and punishment                                           | People are less likely to violate security policies when they believe violations will be detected and punished. |
|   Economic Theory of IS Security    | Costs, benefits, risks, and incentives                                  | Organizations make security decisions by weighing the costs of controls against expected security benefits.     |

1. Systems Thinking
Focus: The whole security ecosystem. Instead of looking at one component, Systems Thinking looks at:
* People
* Technology
* Policies
* Processes
* Management
* External threats

- Main Question is How do all parts of the system interact to produce security outcomes?
- Example:
A ransomware attack occurs.

Systems Thinking asks:
* Was user training adequate?
* Were backups available?
* Were patches applied?
* Was management involved?
* Were warning signs ignored?

The focus is on   relationships and interactions.

2. Socio-Technical Systems Theory
- Focus: People and technology working together.  
This theory assumes that neither technology nor people alone can create security.

- Main Question is Are the human and technical systems properly aligned?
- Example

An organization deploys Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
Socio-Technical Theory asks:
* Can employees use MFA easily?
* Does MFA fit work processes?
* Do users understand its purpose?
* Does it improve security without reducing productivity?

The focus is on the interaction between humans and technology  .

A. (Bostrom & Heinen Model)
Focus: This theory focuses on the joint interaction between:
- People (users, attackers, IT staff)
- Technology (systems, tools, security controls)
- Structure (management, policies, hierarchy)
- Tasks (work processes and goals)

Security is NOT just technical — it emerges when all four components are aligned.
B. Model Diagram

                STRUCTURE
        (Policies, Management, Rules)
                 ↑     ↓

   PEOPLE  ↔  TECHNOLOGY  ↔  TASKS
 (Users)       (Systems)     (Work goals)
                 ↓     ↑

C. How it explains IS Security
A cybersecurity incident happens when there is misalignment:
* Strong firewall (technology)
* Weak training (people)
* Poor policies (structure)
* High workload pressure (tasks)

Result: user bypasses security controls → breach occurs

D. Critical Analysis (Modern IS Security)
Strengths:
* Very relevant for modern hybrid work environments
* Explains human error, insider threats, phishing
* Supports security awareness programs
* Recognizes usability vs security conflict

E. Weaknesses
* Hard to measure “alignment” scientifically
* Overly broad (everything becomes part of the system)
* Less predictive (explains problems better than predicting them)

F. Modern relevance
Highly applicable to:
* Cloud security
* Remote work security
* Zero Trust environments
* Insider threat management

3. Activity Theory
Focus: Human activities and work practices.
Activity Theory examines what people are trying to achieve and how security affects their work.
- Main Question is How do users interact with tools, rules, and communities while performing tasks?
- Example
An employee stores passwords in a spreadsheet.

Activity Theory asks:
* What task was the employee trying to complete?
* What tools were available?
* What rules existed?
* Why did the employee bypass secure methods?

The focus is on   real-world human behavior during work activities  .

A. (Engeström Model)
Focus: Focuses on human activity in context, especially:
> How people use tools under rules, community pressure, and division of labor.
It explains real-world behavior, not just system design.

B. Model Diagram

                 OBJECT (Goal)
                      ▲
                      │
 SUBJECT ─── TOOLS ───┼─── OUTCOME
 (User)      (Tech)   │
                      ▼

 RULES ─── COMMUNITY ─── DIVISION OF LABOUR

C. Meaning of components
* Subject → user (employee, student, hacker)
* Object → goal (send email, access data)
* Tools → systems (email, apps, security tools)
* Rules → policies (password rules, security policies)
* Community → organization environment
* Division of Labour → roles (IT vs users)
* Outcome → result (secure or breached system)

D. How it explains IS Security
Security failures happen due to contradictions:
Example contradiction:
* Employee goal: finish work quickly
* Security rule: verify every attachment

Conflict = unsafe shortcut behavior

D. Critical Analysis
Strengths
* Excellent for explaining user behavior
* Strong in phishing, password misuse, social engineering
* Captures work pressure vs security tension

Weaknesses
* Complex model (hard to apply in practice)
* Weak focus on technical system design
* Difficult to quantify contradictions

Modern relevance
Very strong for:
* Phishing attacks
* BYOD (Bring Your Own Device)
* Remote work behavior
* Security compliance research

4. General Deterrence Theory (GDT)
Focus: Compliance through punishment.
This theory assumes people make decisions based on perceived consequences.

- Main Question is Will the fear of punishment discourage security violations?
- Example
Employees are prohibited from sharing passwords.

GDT asks:
* Are violations monitored?
* Will employees be caught?
* How severe is the punishment?
* Do employees believe punishment will occur?

The focus is on   deterring undesirable behavior  .

A. (Straub Model)
Focus
Focuses on preventing misuse through fear of punishment
> People avoid wrongdoing if they believe they will be caught and punished.

B. Straub Model Diagram

 SECURITY CONTROLS
 (Monitoring, Logging)
          ↓
 PERCEIVED CERTAINTY
 (Chance of being caught)
          ↓
 PERCEIVED SEVERITY
 (How harsh punishment is)
          ↓
      COMPLIANCE
 (Reduced violations)

C. Key variables
* Certainty → Will I get caught?
* Severity → What punishment will I get?
* Celerity → How fast will I be punished?

D. How it explains IS Security
Employees may:
* Share passwords
* Click phishing links
* Bypass controls

IF:
* Monitoring is weak
* Punishment is unclear
* Enforcement is inconsistent

then violations increase

E. Critical Analysis
Strengths
* Simple and practical
* Useful for policy enforcement
* Works in corporate governance

Weaknesses
* Assumes humans are rational (often false)
* Ignores emotions, fatigue, and mistakes
* Weak against external attackers (hackers don’t fear internal punishment)
* Limited effectiveness for insider threats in modern culture

F. Modern relevance
Still used in:
* Security policies
* Compliance enforcement
* Audit systems
* Corporate governance

BUT:
Less effective alone in modern cybersecurity → must be combined with awareness training and usability design.


5. Economic Theory of IS Security
- Focus: Costs, benefits, risk, and investment decisions.  
Security is viewed as an economic decision.

- Main Question Is the investment in security worth the cost?
- Example
A company considers spending $1 million on a new security solution.

Economic Theory asks:
* How much risk will it reduce?
* What losses can be prevented?
* Is the investment financially justified?
* Is there a cheaper alternative?

The focus is on   optimizing security investments and resource allocation  .

A. (Ross Anderson – Security Economics)
Focuses on: Security as a problem of cost, incentives, and resource allocation

Security decisions are economic decisions.
B. Core Model
 COST OF SECURITY CONTROL
          vs
 EXPECTED LOSS FROM ATTACK

or:
Risk = Probability × Impact

B. Key idea (Ross Anderson)
Organizations do NOT fail because of lack of technology.
They fail because:
* Security incentives are misaligned
* Costs are externalized
* People underinvest in protection

C. How it explains IS Security
Example:
A hospital does not invest in:
* MFA
* Staff training
* Advanced email filtering

Why?
* Too expensive
* Benefits are not immediate
* Risk is underestimated

After ransomware attack → huge financial loss

D. Critical Analysis
A. Strengths
* Very realistic for business decisions
* Strong for budgeting and risk analysis
* Explains underinvestment in cybersecurity
* Useful for policy and governance

C. Weaknesses
* Reduces human behavior to money decisions
* Ignores culture and psychology
* Hard to predict attacker behavior
* Does not explain accidental errors well

D. Modern relevance
Extremely important for:
* Cyber insurance
* Risk management
* Security investment decisions
* Enterprise cybersecurity strategy

``Quick Memory Trick``
Think of each theory as looking at cybersecurity through a different lens:

| Theory                             | What It Primarily Looks At |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
|   Systems Thinking                 | The whole system           |
|   Socio-Technical Systems Theory   | People + Technology        |
|   Activity Theory                  | Human work activities      |
|   General Deterrence Theory        | Punishment and compliance  |
|   Economic Theory of IS Security   | Cost, risk, and value      |

- One Security Incident, Five Different Questions
Imagine an employee clicks a phishing email.
*   Systems Thinking:   What system failures allowed this to happen?
*   Socio-Technical Systems Theory:   Was there a mismatch between the user and the security technology?
*   Activity Theory:   What work activity led the employee to click the email?
*   General Deterrence Theory:   Did the employee fear consequences for unsafe behavior?
*   Economic Theory of IS Security:   Was enough investment made in training and security controls?

These different questions show exactly what each theory focuses on.
Final Comparison (Exam-Ready)

| Theory                          | Main Focus                        | Core Question                     | Modern Strength                        |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Socio-Technical Systems         | People + Tech + Structure + Tasks | Are system components aligned?    | Very strong in modern enterprises      |
| Activity Theory                 | Human actions in context          | Why do users behave unsafely?     | Strong for phishing & usability issues |
| General Deterrence Theory       | Punishment & compliance           | Will users fear consequences?     | Moderate (needs support)               |
| Economic Theory (Ross Anderson) | Cost & incentives                 | Is security worth the investment? | Very strong in management decisions    |

Final Critical Insight
Modern cybersecurity problems cannot be explained by only ONE theory:

* Systems Theory → explains system-wide failures
* Socio-Technical → explains misalignment
* Activity Theory → explains user behavior
* Deterrence Theory → explains compliance
* Economic Theory → explains investment decisions

That is why researchers combine them to fully understand cyber security incidents like phishing, ransomware, 
and insider threats.

Case Study: Ransomware Attack at a Hospital
Background
A large hospital relies on an Information System (IS) to manage:
* Patient records
* Medical equipment
* Billing systems
* Staff communication
* Appointment scheduling

One day, a staff member receives a phishing email disguised as a patient document. 
The employee opens the attachment, which installs ransomware. The attackers encrypt patient records and demand payment. 
The hospital cannot access critical information for several days, causing delays in patient care and financial losses.

Applying Different IS Security Theories
Although the same incident occurred, each theory explains it differently.

1. Systems Thinking Perspective
Focus: The entire security ecosystem and how its components interact.
How Systems Thinking Sees the Problem?

The ransomware attack was not caused by a single employee mistake.
Instead, it resulted from failures across multiple interconnected parts of the system.

Components Involved
| Component   | Possible Failure                              |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| People      | Employee clicked the malicious email          |
| Technology  | Email filter failed to detect the threat      |
| Processes   | No effective phishing reporting process       |
| Policies    | Security policies not enforced                |
| Management  | Inadequate security oversight                 |
| Environment | Attackers exploited current ransomware trends |

Questions Asked
* How did the email reach the employee?
* Why was the malware not detected?
* Why was the employee vulnerable?
* Were backups available?
* How did management monitor security risks?

Conclusion
The attack occurred because the entire security system failed collectively.

Recommended Solution
* Improve staff training
* Strengthen email security
* Enhance monitoring systems
* Improve incident response processes
* Increase management involvement

2. Socio-Technical Systems Theory Perspective
Focus: The interaction between people and technology.
How Socio-Technical Theory Sees the Problem

Security failed because the social system and technical system were not aligned.
Social System
* Doctors
* Nurses
* Administrative staff
* IT personnel

Technical System
* Email systems
* Antivirus software
* Authentication systems
* Network security tools

Analysis
The hospital may have installed security tools, but employees did not fully understand how to identify phishing emails.

The technology existed, but users could not effectively work with it.
Questions Asked
* Was the email security system user-friendly?
* Did employees receive adequate training?
* Did security controls support daily work activities?
* Were users involved when security solutions were implemented?

Conclusion
The breach resulted from poor alignment between human behavior and security technologies.

Recommended Solution
* Improve usability of security controls
* Increase security awareness training
* Design technology around user needs
* Encourage collaboration between IT and staff

3. Activity Theory Perspective
Focus
Human activities, tools, rules, and work practices.

Activity System
| Component          | Example                                    |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| Subject            | Hospital employee                          |
| Object             | Process patient information quickly        |
| Tools              | Email software and computer system         |
| Rules              | Security policies and email procedures     |
| Community          | Staff, patients, IT department             |
| Division of Labour | IT secures systems; staff provide services |
| Outcome            | Ransomware infection                       |

Analysis
The employee's primary goal was to process patient information efficiently.
The phishing email appeared legitimate and related to daily work activities.

Contradictions Identified
Subject vs Rules
The employee was expected to follow security procedures but also respond quickly to work requests.

Subject vs Tools
The email system did not clearly indicate the message was malicious.

Object vs Security
Productivity demands conflicted with careful security checks.

Questions Asked
* What was the employee trying to achieve?
* How did the work environment influence behavior?
* What tools were used?
* Which rules conflicted with work objectives?

Conclusion
The breach occurred because work practices and security requirements conflicted.

Recommended Solution
* Integrate security into daily workflows
* Reduce conflicts between productivity and security
* Improve tool design
* Clarify responsibilities

4. General Deterrence Theory Perspective
Focus Compliance through the fear of punishment.

How GDT Sees the Problem
Employees may ignore security rules if they believe there are no consequences.

Analysis
The hospital had security policies, but staff may not have taken them seriously because:
* Violations were rarely monitored
* Penalties were unclear
* Enforcement was inconsistent

Questions Asked
* Did employees know the consequences of policy violations?
* Were violations monitored?
* Was punishment certain?
* Was punishment severe enough?

Key Concepts
Certainty
Would unsafe actions be detected?

Severity
Would there be serious consequences?

Celerity
Would punishment occur quickly?

Conclusion
Weak deterrence reduced compliance with security policies.

Recommended Solution
* Monitor policy violations
* Clearly communicate consequences
* Consistently enforce security rules
* Conduct compliance audits

5. Economic Theory of IS Security Perspective
Focus Costs, risks, benefits, and security investment decisions.
How Economic Theory Sees the Problem
The attack occurred because the organization did not invest optimally in cybersecurity.

Analysis
Before the attack:
* Employee training budget was limited
* Advanced email filtering was considered too expensive
* Backup systems were inadequate

Management attempted to reduce costs.
After the attack:
* Ransom payment
* System recovery costs
* Operational disruption
* Reputational damage

The total loss exceeded the cost of preventive controls.
Questions Asked
* What was the cost of prevention?
* What was the expected loss from a ransomware attack?
* Were security investments economically justified?
* Was the risk assessment accurate?

Example
| Item                        | Cost       |
| --------------------------- | ---------- |
| Security awareness training | $20,000    |
| Advanced email filtering    | $50,000    |
| Attack recovery costs       | $1,000,000 |

The hospital saved $70,000 but lost $1,000,000.

Conclusion
The organization underinvested in cybersecurity relative to the risk.

Recommended Solution
* Conduct cost-benefit analysis
* Increase investment in preventive controls
* Prioritize high-impact security measures
* Calculate Return on Security Investment (ROSI)


6. Comparison of Theories Applied to the Same Incident

| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
|   Systems Thinking                 | Entire security system    | Multiple interconnected failures caused the breach                             |
|   Socio-Technical Systems Theory   | People and technology     | Human behavior and security technologies were not aligned                      |
|   Activity Theory                  | Human work activities     | Work demands and security requirements conflicted                              |
|   General Deterrence Theory        | Punishment and compliance | Employees lacked sufficient motivation to comply with security policies        |
|   Economic Theory of IS Security   | Costs and benefits        | The hospital underinvested in security controls compared to the potential risk |

7. Final Insight

Notice how the same ransomware attack has five different explanations :
*   Systems Thinking:   "The system failed."
*   Socio-Technical Theory:   "People and technology were not aligned."
*   Activity Theory:   "Work activities conflicted with security requirements."
*   General Deterrence Theory:   "Policy enforcement and deterrence were weak."
*   Economic Theory:   "Security investment decisions were inadequate."

This is exactly why theories are useful in cybersecurity research: they provide different lenses for understanding 
the same security problem and lead to different recommendations for improvement.
