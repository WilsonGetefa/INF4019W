The South African government does not officially certify or "authorise" specific third-party digital forensic software, such as the Volatility framework. 
Instead of endorsing particular brands, South African law enforcement strictly regulates the processes, methodology, and qualifications required to ensure 
digital evidence is admissible in court.

How the Law Evaluates Forensic Tools
South African legislation—principally the Cybercrimes Act 19 of 2020 and the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act—mandates that all digital forensic tools 
and practices meet specific criteria to be legally valid:
- Integrity and Reliability: The digital forensic tool used (such as Volatility) must preserve the authenticity and integrity of the digital data. It must not alter or 
corrupt the data being investigated.
- Auditable and Repeatable Procedures: According to the South African Police Service (SAPS) guidelines, any actions taken when accessing or extracting evidence must 
be recorded and audited. An independent third party must be able to repeat the forensic process and achieve the exact same result.
- Investigator Authorization: While the tool itself does not require government authorization, the person conducting the investigation must be a certified digital 
forensic examiner or a technical expert officially designated/authorised to assist a police official during search and seizure operations.

Use of Memory Forensics
Because RAM contains volatile data that is easily lost when a device loses power, memory forensics tools like Volatility are widely utilized by incident response teams 
and digital forensic practitioners to uncover runtime artifacts (e.g., active network connections, running processes, injected malware). While investigators in 
South Africa frequently use this open-source tool for memory analysis, it is simply the chosen medium to access the digital evidence. The admissibility of that evidence 
always relies on the examiner's ability to prove strict adherence to the chain of custody and accepted forensic methodologies.

For official rules and standard operating procedures regarding evidence collection, you can review the SAPS Standard Operating Procedures on Cybercrimes.
